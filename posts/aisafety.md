_"Preface: The overwhelming majority of the people who partake in the AI Safety/EA community are good people. In fact, this year I joined an AI Safety Fellowship at my university and met very talented folks, folks that I wouldn't have gotten in contact with otherwise, and for this experience I am very grateful. Thus, whatever I am about to say going forward is not necessarily a reflection about specifically anyone in the community, but rather aims to address the core ideology, beliefs of AI Safety in general, and how I vehemently disagree with most (if not all!) of their points (which I think is fine!)"_

**Layout**: I will respond to many commonly asked questions about AI Safety (embedding my opinions within my responses), and then end with a paragraph or two about why I'm not really a fan of it specifically.

1. Q: AI safety is something we should all be concerned about.

$\quad$ To this I ask, why? Most people when faced with this question pose two new ones:

$\qquad$ 1. Q: AGI has the potential to revolutionize the world.

<div style="padding-left: 60px;">

A: I don't disagree, obviously. AI in my opinion (and the opinions of many) will be the most transformative technology in the history of mankind, because while past technologies such as the steam engine or the cotton mill may have substantially decreased human labor time and allowed for more efficientized production, AI has the potential to get rid of the need for humans to interact in the production process entirely, and in doing so, cause the destruction of the value form itself.

However, when AI Safetyists talk about the need for AI regulation, they are mainly doing so from a place related to what I like to call "human quasi-welfare" :an almost excessive, downright unnecessary, counterproductive, and oftentimes performative care for the wellbeing and the maintenance of the status quo (this thereby extends to humanity as a whole).

Here's the uncomfortable reality. No matter what we do, when AGI arrives, and it will soon, there will correspondingly arrive a necessity for humanity to address the undeniable truth: all humans will instantly be transformed into being worth nothing more than dispensable fodder that simply cannot contribute anything to society in comparison to one trillion GPUs in a Milwaukee datacenter computing in tandem, their movements optimized for the atom. Now, if society did not equate people and their self worth on the value that their labor creates, these developments would hardly matter. In fact, this new epoch of the world relying on solely AI-driven creations would lead to great joy and infinite amounts of happiness for everyone.

But because society **does** evaluate the worth of people based on the outputs of their labor, at least right now, no doubt a great schism will occur in the near future to either reconcile and overcome this perspective entirely or accept the notion that humanity will become increasingly irrelevant in the near ages.

Met with this undeniable truth, it seems to me that for most of these AI Safetyists, getting rid of this system that will cause the most revolutionary change for ordinary people when it comes to AGI is not as big of a priority as is upholding the status quo, i.e. using Capital to further their goals of safeguarding the hegemony of human labor, whilst being unaware that its reproduction itself inevitably leads to AGI and, therefore, the "end of the world". This can easily be demonstrated by their characteristic love for charities, their love for a degenerated version of entryism into frontier companies, and their love for glorified summer camps where they repeat, spread, improve upon their doctrine.

</div>

$\quad$ 2. Q: AI, in specific, can kill us all, and we need to prevent this reality from occuring.

<div style="padding-left: 60px">

AGI can only "kill us" insofar as labor is concerned. Yes, it can and will cause great social upheaval in relation to this, but it will not lead to the destruction of the human race unless very specific situations occur, and these situations are not the fault of the technology itself but rather the systems in place that perpetuate the acceleration of such technologies, in which case it would be more rational to focus on the dismantling of the systems themselves instead of anything else.

Now, onto the question of ASI.

</div>
