_"Preface: The majority of the people who partake in the AI Safety/EA community are well-intentioned people. I know this firsthand because this year I joined an AI Safety Fellowship at my university and met very interesting people I wouldn't have gotten a chance to meet otherwise. So, whatever I am about to say going forward is not necessarily a reflection about anyone specific or a description of the community writ large, but rather I aim to address the core ideology, beliefs of AI Safety in general, and how I vehemently disagree with most (if not all!) of their points (which I think is fine!)"_

**Layout**: I will respond to many commonly given talking points (embedding my opinions within my responses), and then end with a paragraph or two about why I'm not really a fan.

1. _Q: AI safety is something we should all be concerned about._

$\quad$ To this, I ask, why? Most people when faced with my question pose two new ones:

$\qquad$ _1. Q: AGI has the potential to revolutionize the world, and AI Safety is concerned_

$\qquad$ _with managing the effects in a successful way. (this means that it's good)_

<div style="padding-left: 60px;">

A: I don't disagree with the aspect of change, obviously. AI in my opinion (and the opinions of many) will be the most transformative technology in the history of mankind, because while past technologies such as the steam engine or the cotton mill may have substantially decreased human labor time and allowed for more efficientized production, AI has the potential to get rid of the need for humans to interact in the production process entirely, and in doing so, cause the destruction of the value form itself **(see below.)**

Here's the uncomfortable reality. No matter what we do, when AGI arrives there will correspondingly arrive a need for humans to wrestle with the fact that 99.9% of all people will instantly be valued as nothing more than dispensable fodder that simply cannot compete with the genius of one trillion GPUs in a Milwaukee datacenter operating in tandem, their movements optimized for the atom. They will have no valuable labor to offer and sell to the market, and instantly become "worthless". Now, if society did not force people to barter their labor in order to live a good life, the creation and arrival of AGI would not matter. In this scenario AGI would still create new philosophical questions and arguments about the meaning of what it is to be human, as well as massive social/political change, but materially speaking, it would not make a difference.

But because society **does** evaluate the worth of people based on the outputs of their labor, because society indirectly kills those who cannot perform their duties up to a certain standard,
no doubt a great schism will occur in the near future that either will succeed and overcome this market-driven existence entirely, or accept the mass-adoption of AI in all workplaces, and usher in the end of human dominance as we know it.

When AI Safetyists analyze and talk about these scenarios, they are mainly doing so from a place I like to call "human quasi-welfare": an almost excessive, downright unnecessary, and almost always performative care for the wellbeing and maintenance of the status quo (this thereby extends to humanity as a whole). It seems to me that for most of these AI Safetyists, getting rid of the Capital system is too radical of a conclusion in both logic or action. It would foremost harm the interests of the current state and the current ruling class (which many AI safetyists themselves have personal connections to), so instead of theorizing of ways to solve the problem of AGI and labor through actually effective avenues, they will instead happily spread their contrived messaging in all sorts of diverse ways (think: entryism into frontier labs, unsuccessful charities, meaningless reading groups).

In order to achieve successful change at **ANYTHING**, you must first find a way to enact this change. The method to change the thing MUST be impervious to the thing's wants and needs (which, most of the time, does NOT want to be changed), or otherwise, the thing one wants to change will adapt, overcome, and win against the strategy that works within the thing's confines. There is a concrete reason as to why you won't find virtually any instances of successful peaceful protests against the Nazis in the 30s and 40s (the White Rose incident), or why you will find that the DSA and "No King's Protests" have been UTTER failures at implementing systematic change within the United States government.

Unfortunately, that also means that the current method of spreading the ideology of AI Safety has been an absolute nothingburger, because it denies the existence of a connection between positive change and radical action entirely.

</div>

$\qquad$ _2. Q: AI can easily kill us all, and we need to prevent this reality from occuring by_

$\qquad$ _adopting the thoughts and principles behind AI safety._

<div style="padding-left: 60px">

AGI can only "kill us" insofar as labor is concerned. Yes, it can and will lead to great social upheaval (as I said **above**), but it will not directly lead to the destruction of the human race unless very specific situations allow it to gain access to dangerous or consequential information (such as AGI getting ahold of nuclear codes somehow); these situations are not necessarily the fault of the technology itself but rather the systems in place that perpetuate the platforming of such technologies, in which case it would be more rational to focus on the dismantling of the systems themselves instead of anything else (which contemporary AI Safety does not do).

Now, onto the question of ASI. ASI **does** have the potential to trivially wipe out all of life. It can do this because definitionally, ASI presupposes that its intellect and ability vastly transcends that of humanity, which then allows it to do crazy things, such as build monolith war machines with ease or develop super powerful bioweapons that have both high spread and fatality rates.

A common line of reasoning AI Safetyists have come up with to address the possibility of these scenarios is called AI "misalignment", where an ASI whose goals are "misaligned" with humanity's can lead it to intentionally wreaking immense destruction and suffering across the world. Large numbers of AI Safetyists think that misalignment is a unique property of ASI **onto** us, and that only ASI has the capability to be misaligned. I don't think that is necessarily true.

Humans can just as easily be misaligned to the goals of Life, to the prosperity of existence and sentience beyond this Earth: this is actually the more likely possibility. After all, if ASI is so much more capable and superior to all of humanity, it is also better than us at philosophy, better than us at assessing morals and evaluating the human species relative to the ultimate goal of spreading life, etc. Wouldn't an ASI, after learning about the massive amounts of tragedies committed in the name of "advancing our species" in comparison to our relatively sparse list of genuine achievements and successes, deem us misaligned? How many unarmed forests have we razed in the name of expansion and desire for production? How many slaves have existed for the sole purpose of perpetual servitude in history? Even our so called liberal democracy is full of blatant contradictions and injustices, but no one seems to care nor do anything concrete because of rampant American individualism.

ASI will (rightly) judge us as unworthy of bearing the torch of intelligence any longer, and at that point the field of AI Safety will become entirely worthless because it does not possess any logical way of preventing this future from occurring.

For fun, I will also pretend that I am an AI Safetyist and logically break the problem of "alignment" down. Since I (for the duration of this thought experiment) hold the traditionalist AI Safety view, I agree that methods to prevent misalignment are necessary, which means that we need:

1. A reliable way to hinder the capabilities of ASI without it getting mad or it feeling like we are limiting its autonomy

2. Stemming from the point above, if we are on the intelligence level of an AGI and ASI is a superintelligent being, making this method of hinderance is logically IMPOSSIBLE (because, if we were to successfully create a way of "chaining" a God, then the thing we chained isn't a God, and the ASI isn't an ASI)

3. If you assume that the ASI is "spiky" - meaning that it is ASI in some fields, and below ASI in other ones - then a method still wouldn't be possible because in order to stop an adversary in some specific field, one needs a **better** understanding of the field than the adversary in order to "trap" it or "enclose" it somehow, which contradicts the characterization that it is ASI

So in all cases, preventing "misalignment" against an ASI is impossible, found within the definition.

</div>

_2. I think that the probability of doom through ASI is so high that we should funnel massive quantities of resources in stopping this future due to its EV._

<div style="padding-left: 30px">

The problem with utilizing expected value is that it requires a uniform UNIT of value, a concrete thing RELATIVE to the units of value lost or gained to function as a reference point, and a mathematical framework to MEASURE probability and chance for the whole thing to work. Expected value calculations work for, say, gambling because we have a set unit (a form of currency), a comparative object (my wallet), and a way to measure chance (classical statistics and the impermeable nature of theoretical dice): it completely falls short whenever it lends itself to generalization.

Measure: the probability of dice is constant throughout repeated trials which makes it highly useful as a point of reference to base decisions off of. However, a form of measure is exceedingly difficult to conjure up for world-historic events such as "$P(\mathrm{doom})$" because there is no benchmark for prior, similar events nor is there really a way to gain feedback (the world will end if the event occurs making determination impossible). Some keen readers might point out that there exists a "discrepancy" between $P(\mathrm{doom})$ being uncomputable and my earlier claim that "ASI will come and judge". Of course, my claim is conditional (if ASI becomes true, then...) so I'm not really giving any assertions about decimal probabilities or anything.

What is a "util"? Many people insist that it is a form of representation that is both abstract and adaptable to scenarios, which is a fair response, but I think it still fails to address the addition of utils across different measures. For instance, if we are measuring the value of an action and the action affects a species and the GDP, and we have two representations of value for the species and the GDP respectively (lets call this S and G), how should we aggregate them in a holistic, principled way? What if a species gets $-10S$ but the GDP gains $+10G$? How do we reliably compare against different forms of value and utils? You also can't just brush off this problem by stating that they're all "one" unit because that's inherently circular reasoning, and fails to answer the point.

The "relative" aspect is trivial (relative to what? and a loss relative to nothing is zero.).

If I do assume that EV calculations still work (they don't) in the real world, then my point still stands. I have shown that AI Alignment is functionally impossible (above), so it has a probability of $0\%$ of occurring. I have also shown that the tractability (value, in this case) is functionally $0\%$, because capital as a process cannot pause due to anything AI Safetyists throw at it, so $0 * 0 = 0$. One, by definition, cannot chain a god and still call the thing chained a god.

Utility, and other opaque identifiers of bourgeois values, are all consequences of a disease of moralism and should be given little to no thought as a serious endeavour.

</div>

_3. Alignment is possible._

<div style="padding-left: 30px">

Alright, I'll bite, how?

$\quad$ _1. It is a technical problem, and we can align AI models through mech interp._

<div style="padding-left: 45px">

This can be answered by (1.2) above (we cannot align a "God"), but I can try a different approach.

Are you aware that the modern Neural Network, whilst derived from Perceptrons, actually inherited most of its analytical methods from the study of Sherrington-Kirkpatrick models and spin-glass networks? Are you aware that cracking the SK model took the entire statistical mechanics community decades, which eventually culminated in a proof that showed the free energy of a spin glass was $100\%$ correct, only in 2006? Are you also aware that the SK model is an **insanely** oversimplified version of the modern Neural Network; it, whilst consisting of nodes and edges, doesn't use any non-linear activations, it has no concept of attention, it has no residual connections, and there are no layers upon layers of thousands of nodes all simultaneously acting upon the nodes before and themselves and the future?

Now, in the modern decoder-only transformer there are at LEAST two NNs per one block (the attention head and the FFN, at least because multi-headed attention exists, which can significantly boost this number). Modern LLMs (like Fable) have been estimated to hold $128$ transformer blocks. I'm sure you can start to see just how difficult of a challenge instilling our values and traditions into a model of this caliber can be. I would genuinely be surprised if scientists could reconcile the contradictions between the NTK and feature learning or give an answer as to why benign overfitting is a thing in less than $500$ years.

These questions simply need more advanced math than we have right now, thus, it is a ridiculous pipe dream to assume that alignment is possible. Alignment, in my opinion, necessitates actually going inside the models and changing things about them which is impossible to do in a rigorous way if we do not know how they fundamentally operate. Why do we have to directly go inside of them and see what they are saying in order for alignment to work? Because otherwise, the models could just lie: and we can only determine the existence of lying by looking at if the activations point towards lying. We cannot reliably see if they are lying if we can only observe the outside.

</div>

Also, there are a whole [host](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18397) of problems related to RLHF that I won't get into in this post. And what does indirect even mean? You can either talk to the models directly or look inside of them (which we have determined **above** cannot be representative of the entire picture).

**The effects of ASI cannot be reconciled,** nor can it be transitioned "smoothly" into a world where humans can co-exist. This is an inevitable and disappointing axiom which I hold to be infinitely true, for an infinite amount of time.

But AGI can. AGI can be integrated with humanity and is positioned perfectly to disrupt the movement that is Capital recursion. Let us ruminate over two possibilities that could equally occur: either AGI has the capability to produce socially necessary labor time, or it doesn't.

In the first possibility, AGI is seen as so efficient that firms are downright bought off if they refuse to adopt this technology top-down. After all, who wouldn't want a worker that needs neither sleep nor sustenance? So, workers are let go one by one as firms start producing commodities with AI on an unparalleled level. Due to the ability that AI agents have to efficiently organize and optimize themselves much better than any group of humans can, commodities get imbued with less and less socially necessary labor time during the production process. This holds true for all commodities, as the inefficiencies and asymmetry found within human-powered labor, as well as the transitions between raw material to machinery all become negligible differences. Because workers are cut-off from this process they are consequently cut off from receiving any wages and thus cannot engage with the market. The market morphs into a closed-loop, where the only interaction happening is between firms and other firms, driven by the owners and the agents in direct communication with one another. The working class, unable to receive any means to continue living or perform their livelihood, peer into the veil of the market with incredulous disbelief: how can the contradictions of capital get more blatant than this? Either: A. There are riots overnight, and the working class get their due; B. The ruling class implement pseudo-UBI, but this can only be interpreted as an early admission of defeat, an empty platitude and baseless compromise, so we get the same end result; C. The ruling class, in desperation, enact mass violence against the citizens of the world, attempting to prevent a sudden loss of power, and push all their resources towards building ASI. The creation of ASI **must** happen in a quick manner, because technology has its internal limits in stopping the full force of billions of people. Now when ASI arrives, it cannot be anything but moral, so in the small chance they succeed the entire human race is wiped out. In the more likely scenario, the fascistic regime (that has no reproduction mechanism, no internal stability, and finite resources) is destroyed by revolt or revolution. Thus, socialism is implemented in all scenarios where humanity still exists.

For the second possibility (which assumes that AI **cannot** create socially necessary labor time), firms are nevertheless incentivized to adopt AI regardless of if they believe the LTV is true or not. The same events occur as in the previous scenario until surplus value is involved. Because in this world we have assumed AI cannot create SNLT, the value of commodities drop to zero, which effectively kills the rate of profit and forces it to go to zero as well. Now the system has to sustain itself under a two-pronged attack: the very palpable anger at the hands of the now disenfranchised working class, and the structural inability to continue the ideology of Capital any longer. Met with these two forces it is near trivial to see how easily the system will collapse.

So to finally answer the question of why I am not a fan of orthodox AI Safety? It can simply be deduced by my answers of the questions above. The ideology is inherently for a "smooth" transition, it is performative, it is brittle and permeable to criticism, and it has constantly changing definitions for jargon and terminology. I am saddened to see the rise of its adoption in frontier labs and AI circles, and although I am aware of its purported rise I still hope that this is the peak of the popularity of the movement.
