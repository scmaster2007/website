title: Proof of the existence of TzimTzum
date: 2026-06-03
---

1. Assume that G-D is infinite, ominpotent, and can transcend all things (this is an axiom accepted by many major religions). We also assume, trivially, that everything in the Torah does not contradict itself (we assume this is a logical impossibility, as the Torah is "perfect", according to many major religions).

2. Note that we live in a universe made out of matter. We have two subcases corresponding to the different potential sizes of the universe:

   a. If the universe is truly infinite, then G-D supercedes that "infinity", because we assume He can transcend all things, so it is always true that G-D "encircles" this space. I guess you can think about G-D's cardinality as being "greater" than the cardinality/complexity of the universe.

   b. The universe is finite, in which case G-D being able to encase this space is trivial (he can just be $n+1$ if universe is size $n$).

3. So, it is implied that anything we see and can imagine is encircled by G-D Himself.

4. So either our existence is within:

   a. Some space within G-D, i.e. we are integrated components within his "space", but this contradicts our assumption that G-D can transcend all things, as if we are within Him He is not transcended but rather on the same plane of material existence as mortals, so this cannot be true for the assumption to be true.

   b. Or, there exists some space created by G-D, that is separate from His nature but also derived from his powers, where we reside. Essentially, the complement of the set of G-D that G-D also created.

5. So, it is implied that we are in this space created by G-D, because we know a. to be false.

6. Take the first few passages of Genesis. It is stated that there existed nothing but "formlessness and emptiness, darkness". Because G-D is omnipotent and the creator of all things, and because this space has to be derived from G-D, we can assume that G-D created this "darkness" from within himself. Additionally, G-D also emanated the light from Himself because He has to have created everything (assumption is from the Torah, which we have assumed to be true. Perhaps later I can think of a proof from first principles. A thought I have is to start from the assumption of omnipotence, which means that G-D could've created this space, and if he could've, he would've, because he is infinite).

7. The world is, according to that passage, derived from this synthesis between the darkness and light. Genesis 1:4, "God saw that the light was good, and He separated the light from the darkness".

8. So we have proved that, the world in which we exist is separate from G-D but also derived from His powers, that this world was created through the meticulous interaction between Light and Darkness ("separated the light and dark").

9. We have also proved that the universe we live in is "finite" relative to G-D's infinity, because he encircles everything. So because this universe is finite, G-D must have had to, in the process of creation, utilize His "infinity" to transform to the "finite", and manipulate this "finite" in such a way that results in the current space we reside in.

10. So, there exists some point in time, or some point in G-D's number line when we can "transition" from "infinite" to "finite", which further necessitates the subcomponents of this light into different pieces in order to make it fininte.

11. So, finally, we have proved the necessity of TzimTzum, as it relates to analyzing and understanding the transition between the infinite and finite, given our priors.
